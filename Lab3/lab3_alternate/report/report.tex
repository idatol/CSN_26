\section{Introduction}

We study the global syntactic dependency network of ten languages from the Universal
Dependencies (UD) collection. Vertices are words and two words are linked if they formed a
syntactic dependency at least once in the treebanks of the language. We use the undirected
version of these networks.

The metric of interest is the mean closeness centrality
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{C}_i,
  \qquad
  \mathcal{C}_i = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{\substack{j=1\\ j\neq i}}^{N}\frac{1}{d_{ij}},
\end{equation}
where $d_{ij}$ is the geodesic distance between $i$ and $j$ (the term is $0$ when $j$ is
unreachable from $i$, so all $\mathcal{C}_i$ are finite and $0\le \mathcal{C}_i\le 1$).

We ask whether $\mathcal{C}$ is \emph{significantly large}, i.e.\ whether
$p(\mathcal{C}_{NH}\ge\mathcal{C})$ is small, under two null hypotheses: a binomial
(Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi) graph with the same $N$ and $E$, and a random graph with the same degree
sequence as the real network (switching model). The $p$-values are estimated with a Monte Carlo
procedure. \todo{one or two sentences announcing the main finding once results are in.}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Network construction}

The networks are built from the UD treebanks v2.18 for the same ten languages as in Lab~2
(script \texttt{code/build\_networks.R}). For each language, all its treebanks are pooled,
except four that were excluded: UD\_Arabic-NYUAD and UD\_English-GUMReddit (no word forms),
UD\_Italian-Old (historical language) and UD\_Chinese-GSDSimp (same text as Chinese-GSD).

\begin{itemize}
  \item Vertices are lowercased word forms. In the files, the two words of an edge
        are separated by a tab, since a few forms contain spaces; the program checks the header
        ($N$, $E$) against the file when loading it.
  \item For every token whose head is not the root, the pair $\{$head, dependent$\}$ is an
        edge. Edges involving punctuation (UPOS \texttt{PUNCT}) are ignored.
  \item Direction is discarded: each pair is stored once in canonical order, so that
        head$\to$dependent and dependent$\to$head are the same edge.
  \item Loops are removed and repeated edges are counted once. Pairs are put in canonical
        order with byte-wise (locale-independent) string comparison.
\end{itemize}

$N$ counts the words that appear in at least one edge. Each network is written to
\texttt{data/language\_dependency\_network.txt} (first line: $N\ E$; then one edge per line).
Note that these networks are unions of many sentence trees; they are not trees and contain
cycles.

\subsection{Computing the mean closeness centrality}

The network is stored as an adjacency list in compressed sparse row form. $\mathcal{C}_i$ is
obtained with a breadth-first search (BFS) from $i$, adding $1/d_{ij}$ for each vertex reached.
Isolated vertices have $\mathcal{C}_i=0$ and need no search. The code is written in C++17 and
compiled with \texttt{-O3}; BFS runs, and independent random replicates, are parallelised with
OpenMP (8 threads).

\subsection{Null models and Monte Carlo estimation}

The $p$-value is estimated as $p(\mathcal{C}_{NH}\ge\mathcal{C})\approx f/T$, where $T$ is the
number of random graphs generated and $f$ the number of them with
$\mathcal{C}_{NH}\ge\mathcal{C}$. Comparisons use a tolerance of $10^{-12}$ to avoid
floating-point order effects. We use $T=100$ random graphs per null model, except for Czech
($T=50$); the values are listed in Table~\ref{tab:pvalues}.

\paragraph{Binomial graph.} A graph with $N$ vertices and $E$ edges: $E$ distinct pairs of
distinct vertices are drawn uniformly, rejecting loops and repeated pairs.

\paragraph{Switching model.} Starting from the real edge list, we perform $QE$ attempts. Each
attempt picks two edges $u\sim v$ and $s\sim t$ uniformly. The second edge is flipped
(exchange $s$ and $t$) with probability $1/2$ and the edges are rewired to $u\sim t$ and
$s\sim v$; the flip makes the two possible rewirings equally likely, since the stored order of
the endpoints of an edge is arbitrary. Attempts that are not valid are skipped, but still count
towards the $QE$ attempts. Every replicate starts again from the original network.

\emph{Which switchings preserve the degree sequence?} Both rewirings of two edges
$u\sim v$, $s\sim t$, namely $\{u\sim t, s\sim v\}$ and $\{u\sim s, v\sim t\}$, preserve the
degree sequence, because each vertex loses and gains exactly one edge.

\emph{Which of them produce forbidden edges?} A rewiring creates a loop if the two new
endpoints coincide, e.g.\ $u=t$ (or $s=v$) for $\{u\sim t, s\sim v\}$. It creates a multiedge
if a new edge already exists in the graph. Edges that share a vertex (e.g.\ $u=s$) are the
typical case: the rewiring then either reproduces an existing edge or does nothing useful, and
is rejected by these two checks. Choosing the same edge twice is also rejected.

\paragraph{Choice of $Q$.} By the coupon collector argument, about $\ln E$ rounds are needed
for every edge to have been touched at least once, so we use $Q=\max(10,\lceil\ln E\rceil)$
(this gives $Q=11$ for Hungarian, $12$ for Basque, Chinese and Greek, $13$ for Arabic, Catalan,
English, Italian and Turkish, and $15$ for Czech). \todo{Report the fraction of attempts that
were accepted.}

\subsection{Exact computation with early stopping}

To decide whether $\mathcal{C}_{NH}\ge\mathcal{C}$ it is not always necessary to compute
$\mathcal{C}_{NH}$ fully. Vertices are visited in a uniformly random order, and after $M$
vertices bounds on $\mathcal{C}_{NH}$ are available (we write $S_M$ for the sum of the computed
$\mathcal{C}_i$):
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{C}_{NH}^{\min} &= \frac{S_M}{N}
  &&\text{(unvisited vertices contribute 0)},\\
  \mathcal{C}_{NH}^{\max} &= \frac{1}{N}\Big(S_M+\sum_{i\ \text{unvisited}} u_i\Big),
  &&u_i=\frac{k_i + (N-1-k_i)/2}{N-1}.
\end{align}
The upper bound is tighter than the generic $\mathcal{C}_i\le1$: a vertex of degree $k_i$ has at
most $k_i$ vertices at distance 1 and every other vertex is at distance at least 2 (and
unreachable vertices count as 0). Isolated vertices have $u_i=0$ and are never visited. The
search stops with ``$\ge$'' as soon as $\mathcal{C}_{NH}^{\min}\ge\mathcal{C}$ and with ``$<$''
as soon as $\mathcal{C}_{NH}^{\max}<\mathcal{C}$. This gives exactly the same answer as the full
computation. It saves much work when $\mathcal{C}_{NH}$ is clearly below $\mathcal{C}$, but little
when it is above (nearly all vertices must be visited before the lower bound reaches
$\mathcal{C}$). The shortcut for degree-one vertices ($d_{ij}=d_{kj}+1$) was not used: it
requires the BFS of the neighbour $k$ anyway.

\subsection{Approximate computation}

Exact computation costs about $N\cdot E$ operations per graph, which is not feasible for the
largest networks (e.g.\ Czech: $N=215082$, $E=1.9\cdot10^{6}$). For those we estimate
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}\approx\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{C}_i
\end{equation}
over $M$ vertices chosen uniformly at random for each random graph (no early stopping). The
mean closeness of the \emph{real} network is always computed exactly (one BFS from every vertex,
parallelised over 8 threads), so the only sampling error comes from the random
graphs. The cost is proportional to $M/N$.

Languages with $N\cdot E<10^{10}$ (Arabic, Basque, Catalan, Chinese, Greek, Hungarian) were
processed exactly (with early stopping). English, Italian and Turkish used $M/N=10\%$ and
Czech $M/N=1\%$. To assess the effect of the approximation, Hungarian was run exactly and with
decreasing $M/N$ (Table~\ref{tab:validation}). The $p$-value for the binomial model is $0$ in
all cases, but the switching $p$-value decreases as $M/N$ decreases, i.e.\ the approximation is
biased towards smaller $p$-values.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

Each replicate uses its own pseudo-random stream seeded from the global seed and the replicate
index, so results do not depend on the number of threads. The seed is $1$ for all runs.
$T$, $Q$ and $M/N$ per language are given above and in Table~\ref{tab:pvalues}. Everything is
reproduced from the repository root with
\begin{verbatim}
Rscript code/build_networks.R ../Lab2/ud-treebanks-v2.18 data   # networks
make                                                            # compile
./run_all.sh                                                    # Table 2
./validate_approx.sh Hungarian 100                              # Table 3
\end{verbatim}
